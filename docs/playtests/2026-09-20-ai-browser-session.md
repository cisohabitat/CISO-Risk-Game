## Answers to the protocol's five questions

### 1. Where did understanding break down?

The most consequential uncertainty was the distinction between present workload capacity and accumulated team health. The final headline made sense as a possible organisational outcome, but this run's main briefing did not make that outcome salient enough for the player to anticipate it.

Other concrete rereading points were recruitment overlap, board-agenda eligibility, programme completion versus operational assurance, the finance percentages, and assumption reminders with no recorded assumptions.

### 2. Where did engagement decline?

This cannot measure human boredom. The observed proxy was reduced exploration and increased reliance on Skip ahead. Repeated risk descriptions were increasingly skimmed from May. The clearest disengagement was the late-August-to-September stretch after identity enforcement. October's restore and budget decisions restored interest. November and December again relied on self-directed investigation rather than a strong narrative push.

The evidence does not support saying Q4 was wholly empty. Two decisions in that quarter were clearly connected to the player's year. It does support testing whether their follow-up and the final stretch are sufficiently prominent.

### 3. What was missed?

- Thin service-review result, discovered late.
- Draft supplier hypothesis awaiting promotion, left for almost two game months.
- Positive identity-enforcement follow-up, discovered only during a deliberate late inbox review.
- Repeated drift and assumption-review notices.
- Any sustained cue that Cyber Risk and Engineering morale were deteriorating despite apparent workload relief.
- Most routine inbox messages and some urgent messages outside the main decision queue.

Pattern offers were noticed, but many were left untouched. The first supplier offer read as a meaningful insight. Later offers increasingly resembled additional work competing with the visible decision queue. Only one offer was shown at a time in the inspected briefing states, so this session does not directly test choosing among several simultaneously visible offers.

### 4. Which decisions caused hesitation?

1. Day 40 capacity relief: already-funded programmes versus immediate spend versus people.
2. Day 120 SOC renewal: preferred action unaffordable, replacement expensive and disruptive.
3. Day 200 acquisition: business value versus an unknown connected estate, with quarantine unaffordable.
4. Day 275 recovery: meaningful assurance versus business interruption and insufficient COO support.

Several other decisions were easier because one answer was visibly better aligned with responsible practice. The difficult decisions were those constrained by the player's previous commitments.

### 5. Did the annual review match the player's account?

Mostly on capability, evidence gaps, detection and business friction. Not on the dominant people outcome. The review's specificity was useful, but the sustainability headline and some priority attribution felt less well connected to what the player had understood during play.

## Known-weakness entries for maintainer review

These are prepared in the protocol's observation-count-suggestion form. They are proposed entries, not changes made to the repository. No balance or content changes were made from this single run.

- **Capacity relief can be mistaken for team recovery.** In one AI-controlled browser session, the player bought capacity, recruited and later saw team capacity as available; the annual review nevertheless led with burnout in Engineering and Cyber Risk. This suggests testing whether cumulative team health needs a clearer briefing cue distinct from current availability. Human confirmation is required.
- **Decision-led navigation hides consequential follow-ups.** In one AI session, the thin initial enquiry and successful identity-enforcement follow-up were missed until deliberate inbox revisits. This suggests testing whether decision outcomes and commissioned-work results need stronger placement in the briefing or an explicit unread-result cue.
- **Finance copy and amounts did not reconcile.** In one observed state, 15% of £60k remaining was paired with £220k and £110k choices. This warrants checking the displayed amount and basis against the intended rule, without assuming a particular engine defect.
- **Assumption reminder can be unsupported by the workspace.** In one run, an inbox message stated several assumptions were overdue while the Assumptions tab said none were recorded. This warrants checking when the reminder should appear and whether it describes tracked items accurately.
- **Board agenda eligibility was not self-explanatory.** In one run, several risks were visible but only the risk linked to treatment appeared in the first board paper. The player inferred a formalisation requirement later. This suggests testing a contextual explanation or route back to formalise an emerging risk.
- **Completion, delivery confidence and recovery assurance were conflated by the player.** In one run, 100% recovery delivery with good confidence coexisted with Limited recovery confidence; later a separate production-test decision clarified part of the distinction. This suggests testing labels and an explicit assurance explanation, not removing uncertainty.
- **Review narration may need stronger attribution.** In one run, the review called the play backlog-led and highlighted a build-pipeline risk, while the player understood their choices as business-service-led recovery and identity investment. Verify the intended attribution and make the causal explanation visible before changing the scoring.

## What this session does not settle

- Guided naming: not tested. CISO was chosen because it said intended experience.
- High Pressure executive patience and perceived noise: not tested.
- Risk-band retuning: no justification from this session alone. Scenario descriptions were sufficient to prioritise; repeated Moderate labels added little.
- Balance changes, hidden numeric risk disclosure, reduced uncertainty or faster consequences: not recommended on this evidence.
- Human timing: the 14-minute AI run is not a human reading-time benchmark.
- Mobile, iPad, visual layout, accessibility and alternate seeds: not tested in this session.

Recommended next step: run the same protocol with human participants and specifically compare their Q3 interpretation of team health against the final review. Preserve the strong late-year programme decisions and verify the concrete message inconsistencies separately from subjective tuning.
