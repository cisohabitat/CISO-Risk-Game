# Owner-supplied five-run CISO review — 30 October 2026

Supplied in the conversation. Build/commit, seeds, exports, timing and participant
independence were not supplied. This is one supplied account of five campaigns,
not five independent participants or matched difficulty/strategy evidence.

## Supplied account

All five runs together cover every difficulty and scenario. The biggest takeaway
is that the overall rating tracks team burnout and programme delivery much more
than business results, and difficulty barely changes how hard the game plays.

| Run | Difficulty | Scenario | Rating | Objectives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guided | Inherited mess | Credible | 1/5 |
| 2 | CISO | Tidy inheritance | Mixed | 3/5 |
| 3 | High pressure | New money | Credible | 4/5 |
| 4 | Guided | After the breach | Credible | 0/5 |
| 5 | CISO | Surprise me (After the breach) | Mixed | 4/5 |

### Balance

- Business results barely count: you can miss all five objectives and still get
  "Credible" (run 4), while 3 or 4 of 5 with tired staff gets only "Mixed".
- Difficulty doesn't scale: the High pressure run was the smoothest and Guided
  was the harshest. Strategy matters far more than the setting.
- Objectives are invisible: you only see them in the year-end review. "Enter the
  Nordic market" also failed twice for no visible reason, and a 30-day delay
  counted as a miss in some runs and "achieved" in another.
- Burnout can't be fixed: "Recruit properly" (£140k) left vacancies unfilled in
  every run, buying capacity barely helps, and teams stay "burning out" even
  with nothing committed. That lane alone capped two runs.
- Evasive options are too cheap: "Fix it quietly" and "on track" to auditors
  never cost anything. "Note it and keep watching" did cause breaches, but the
  review never ties them back to that choice.
- Budget timing decides the game: spending it all in January locks you out of
  later decisions. Holding a reserve (run 3) was the winning pattern, but nothing
  teaches it.
- The same SOC lesson every time: incidents always come with "two SOC warnings
  never followed up", but the game never gives you a clear way to follow up an
  early warning.

### Bugs in every run

- Hidden December: December events only appear on the year-close screen, so the
  last month is effectively unplayable.
- Inbox inflation: the badge counts reference mail, reaching 170 to 216 unread
  by year end.
- Long gaps: there are two- to six-week stretches with no decisions.
- Wellbeing: the wellbeing panel ignores staffing and capacity.
- Unannounced refunds: budget refunds like "duplicate tool cancelled" turn up
  without notice.

### Other bugs

Board papers:

- Accepted risks are still listed as "Required".
- Papers overlap, so two are always open at once.

Decisions and options:

- "Ninety days, then this question again" comes back early.
- The final priorities option says "You are not running a programme to lead
  with" even after programmes finish.

Programmes, enquiries and assumptions:

- Stopped enquiries keep their cost.
- Paused programmes show as "still running" in the timeline.
- Assumptions can only be marked "still holds".
- Stale briefing cards: old cards sit on the briefing for months.

Fixed since your first playtests:

- Raised risks now stay on board papers.
- Commitment reviews show an outcome.
- Hypotheses and commissioning now give clear confirmations.
- Suggested hypotheses no longer inflate the attention count.

### Top fixes

- Make December playable.
- Add an objectives tracker during the year.
- Weight business outcomes more in the overall rating.
- Make recruitment and capacity actually relieve burnout.
- Give evasive choices visible consequences, named in the review.
- Make High pressure genuinely harder.
